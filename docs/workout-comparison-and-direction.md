# Workout tracker comparison and future direction

This records the owner's Notion workflow, a read-only repository review and online research on 2026-11-08. Product descriptions are based on vendor documentation, not independent hands-on testing. This preserves the pre-implementation review and owner decisions. The subsequent local implementation is described in `setup.md`; the comparison below describes the earlier baseline.

## Previous Notion workflow

The Exercises database contains one row per equipment-specific exercise or meaningful variant. Muscle, session, rotation, role, variant and order properties support multiple views. Round 1 and Round 2 board views group non-hidden fixed and round-specific exercises into days; the owner remembers the next day and round. An exercise page combines its properties, the ten most recent related logs, personal machine setup and an occasional video. Setup is adjusted as experience changes. A single central Logs database stores all performances; it is normally accessed through each exercise's filtered view. The log records reps, weight, notes, a relation and a creation date used as the exercise date.

**Strengths:** exact-machine identity; concise, scannable recent results and qualitative notes; personal, editable setup and history in one place; one central log with useful exercise-specific views; flexible filtering and multi-day reuse; and cloud access with selectable offline availability.

**Limitations:** the owner must remember day and round; global rounds couple otherwise independent rotations; a wide board and separate exercise pages can mean extra phone navigation; routine membership/order attached to an exercise does not naturally express different positions in different routines; created date makes later corrections awkward; compact rep labels can be less useful for analysis than separately stored sets; setup changes are not inherently historical; and Notion does not intrinsically manage a session's plan, skips and substitutions. Notion's offline download has page/database scope and conflict limitations; see [Notion's offline documentation](https://www.notion.com/help/use-pages-offline).

## Other solutions researched

- [FitNotes](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.github.jamesgay.fitnotes) offers custom exercises, named routine days, history while recording, notes, backup and CSV export. Its [exercise guide](http://www.fitnotesapp.com/exercises/) explicitly mentions machine settings in notes. Some advanced tracking types and per-exercise units are Supporter features. Borrow quick exercise-history access and a small personal library; do not assume backup is multi-device sync.
- [Hevy exercise history](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35382889578135-Exercise-Performance-Tracking-in-Library-Weight-Bodyweight-Cardio-and-Duration-Based-Exercises) connects exercise details, performances and workout context. Its [note scopes](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/36011779117591-How-do-the-exercise-notes-routine-and-workout-notes-work) separate pinned exercise, routine and workout notes; [previous values](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/36011896355479-How-to-Use-Previous-Workout-Values-to-Improve-Performance-in-Hevy) can be scoped to all workouts or a routine. Borrow direct navigation and clear note scope, not mandatory set-by-set logging or social features. Its [feature guide](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/33106320824727-Everything-You-Need-to-Know-About-the-Hevy-App-2026-Features-Guide) describes free-tier restrictions.
- [Strong](https://help.strongapp.io/article/134-about-notes) distinguishes pinned exercise reminders and workout-specific notes. Its [CSV export](https://help.strongapp.io/article/235-export-workout-data) cannot be imported back. Keep restorable backup distinct from any future analysis export.
- [Boostcamp](https://www.boostcamp.app/features) describes swapping for one workout or future program weeks. Borrow explicit scope, not a large program catalog, automatic progression or composite strength scores by default.

Feature descriptions, free limits and offline behavior can change. No competitor was installed or independently verified.

## Current app: review, not a specification of future work

Workout starts a suggested remaining routine day, any selected day or a free workout. Each routine has its own day progress; independent exercise cycles can be shared across days and handle out-of-order member selections without skipping earlier suggestions. Building routines is separate from training. The library stores exact variants, setup, instructions, links, tags and archive state. A focused session shows setup and three recent exact-variant results, previous values, one-tick three-set entry, cardio, notes, skips, today-only substitutions and additions. Activity filters workouts by exercise and date, opens workout details, edits existing completed results/notes and offers an optional top-weight chart of the displayed matching workouts. JSON backup/restore is available. Account workspaces are online-only, revision-checked and held in memory; demo is temporary.

**Better than Notion:** next-day suggestions; independent cycles; structured sets displayed compactly; session-specific changes without silently editing routines; stable exercise identity; planned/performed context; and an existing history/correction foundation.

**Gaps:** setup, three recent results and complete history are split across session, library and Activity; no direct exercise jump/overview within a session; automatic completion can end a session as the final result is saved, before its note can be entered or extras added; insights are minimal and chart scope is limited to displayed workouts; date and exercise identity are not generally editable in past results; archived entries remain available in pickers; and full-workspace network writes, save/conflict recovery and live isolation have limitations documented in `setup.md`. This is source/documentation review, not proof of real-service behavior.

## Owner feedback and proposed direction

1. Add controlled expandable views and navigation, preserving existing quick-glance information and quick edits. An exercise-centered view should bring setup, instructions/link and more exact-variant results together, accessible from library, session and history without losing session drafts.
2. Add an optional workout overview for direct navigation/status; do not restore session reordering controls or silently alter the routine.
3. Make final-exercise notes and extras smooth before completion. A brief explicit completion review is a candidate, not yet implemented. Avoid committing cycle progress twice; keep confirmed-result/notes semantics clear.
4. Clarify change scope. Routine structure changes in the builder, while session substitutions/extras are today-only and setup edits persist to the library. Session results/notes and later history corrections also persist as workout records; they do not change the routine. Do not imply setup is the only persistent effect of working out.
5. **Better insights are a major goal**, not a secondary chart polish. Provide useful per-exercise and overall activity insights with understandable definitions, date ranges, textual context and appropriate empty states. Preserve exact machine identity, distinguish reps from load, and avoid cross-machine weight comparisons or invented composite scores. Choose metrics and interaction design based on the available data; do not imply a single weight chart is comprehensive. Keep compact history and quick entry.
6. Add small contextual info/help affordances throughout Workout Tracker where concepts need explaining, without clutter or redundant text. Record the same idea for Habit Tracker later; do not extend this task to the habit UI.
7. Owner never performs partial strength results; keep zero or three sets and one completion action. The prior suggestion about a forgotten exercise was unclear and is not a confirmed need. The owner confirmed that all exercises in a workout are performed on the same day and chose one shared, automatically assigned workout date, editable later; exercise identity and notes should also be correctable in history. Result weights/reps already have correction controls. Corrections should preserve historical facts, prevent accidental reassignment, keep exact-variant history accurate, and not silently recalculate cycle progress or invent historical snapshots.
8. Offline work is deferred by the owner. Account data remains online-only; do not introduce an offline cache or sync redesign as part of this work.

Potential later ideas, not mandatory: duplicate an exercise as a new independent variant; CSV for analysis in addition to restorable JSON; a compact routine overview; searchable exercise pickers; timers and workout-level notes if needed.
